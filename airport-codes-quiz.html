About Airport Codes Quiz

Every airport has a three-letter IATA code, the one printed on your boarding pass and bag tag. Some are easy, like LHR for London Heathrow. Others are puzzles: ORD for Chicago O'Hare, YYZ for Toronto, BOM for Mumbai. Airport Codes Quiz turns those codes into a quick game you can play anywhere.

How a round works

Each question names an airport, such as Tunis Carthage International, and gives you four codes to choose from. Pick one and you'll see straight away whether you were right.

Start each day with the Daily Challenge: 10 questions, the same for every player, worth double XP. Play daily to build your streak and level up. Then pick a mode: Classic for 10 questions at your own pace, Time Attack to answer as many as you can in 60 seconds, or Survival to keep going until you've lost three lives.

Every code you miss goes into My mistakes, so you can go back over just the ones that caught you out. The Learn tab lets you study the codes, and Progress shows how many of the 1,000 airports you've mastered.

Tips for a higher score

  • Many codes simply spell the city: DXB is Dubai, MAD is Madrid.
  • A code starting with Y is almost always in Canada. Here's why.
  • If the letters don't fit, try an old name: PEK is Peking, BOM is Bombay. More old names.
  • Learn the look-alikes, like LHR and LGW, or IAD and IAH. Each of our airport pages lists the codes most often mixed up.
