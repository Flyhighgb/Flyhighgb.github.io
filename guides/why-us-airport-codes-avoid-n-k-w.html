Why US airport codes avoid N, K and W

Newark isn't NEW and Kansas City isn't KAN. A handful of letters are off limits for US airports, and it shows in the codes.

By Amateurgames1 · Published 16 November 2026

Look down a list of US airport codes and you'll notice something missing. Almost none begin with N, K or W, and very few with Q. That's not chance. Those letters were reserved for other uses long before most airports got their codes.

N: kept for the Navy

Codes beginning with N are reserved for the US Navy. That's why Newark couldn't be NEW and became EWR, taking the next letters of its name. Nashville is BNA, from Berry Field, Nashville. New Orleans is MSY, from Moisant Stock Yards. Norfolk, Virginia, is ORF.

K and W: kept for radio and TV

In the US, radio and TV stations have call signs that begin with K or W, roughly K west of the Mississippi and W to the east. To avoid confusion with those call signs, airport codes avoid both letters. That's why Kansas City is MCI, from Mid-Continent International, and why Washington's airports are DCA, IAD and BWI.

K does turn up in US aviation, but in a different place. The four-letter ICAO codes pilots use put a K in front of the US airport's code, so Los Angeles is KLAX. That's covered in IATA and ICAO codes explained.

Q: kept for telecoms

Q is used for international telecoms codes, the "Q codes" used in radio communication. US airports avoid it as a first letter for the same reason.

Outside the US, the rule doesn't apply

These restrictions are American. Elsewhere, codes starting with N, K and W are common:

  • NRT: Tokyo Narita.
  • KIX: Osaka Kansai.
  • KUL: Kuala Lumpur.
  • NBO: Nairobi.
  • KEF: Reykjavík Keflavík.
  • WAW: Warsaw.
  • WLG: Wellington.
  • NCE: Nice.

So if you see a code starting with N, K or W, you can be fairly sure it isn't in the United States. That's a handy shortcut in a quiz.

Canada has a letter of its own

Canada went the other way and put a Y at the front of nearly every major airport code. Toronto is YYZ, Vancouver YVR. Read the story in why Canadian airports start with Y.

How to use this

  • A US city starting with N, K or W will almost always have a code that doesn't start with that letter.
  • Look for the next strong letters in the name, as with nEWaRk, or for an old airport name, as with BNA and MCI.
  • A code starting with N, K or W is a strong hint the airport is outside the US.
