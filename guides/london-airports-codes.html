London has six airports. Can you name their codes?

LHR, LGW, STN, LTN, LCY and SEN, plus the LON code that covers them all, and how not to mix them up.

By Amateurgames1 · Published 5 November 2026 · Updated 16 November 2026

London is served by six airports, and their codes are a classic quiz trap because most start with L. Here they are, with a way to remember each, and how to get from each one into the city.

The six codes

  • LHR Heathrow: L for London, HR for Heathrow. The UK's busiest airport, in west London. The Heathrow Express runs to Paddington, and the Elizabeth line and Piccadilly line also serve it.
  • LGW Gatwick: L for London, GW for Gatwick. South of London, in West Sussex, with one main runway. The Gatwick Express runs to Victoria.
  • STN Stansted: the letters come straight from STaNsted. North-east of London, in Essex, and a big base for low-cost airlines. The Stansted Express runs to Liverpool Street.
  • LTN Luton: LuToN. North of London, in Bedfordshire. Trains stop at Luton Airport Parkway, linked to the terminal by a short shuttle.
  • LCY London City: London CitY. The closest to central London, in the Docklands, with a short runway and a steep approach that only some aircraft can fly. It's on the DLR.
  • SEN Southend: SouthENd, out east in Essex. Its own station has trains to Liverpool Street.

What does LON mean?

LON isn't an airport. It's a city code that covers all of London's airports, so booking sites can search them all at once. Other big cities have one too: NYC for New York, PAR for Paris and TYO for Tokyo. Our guide to city codes lists the rest.

Searching LON is a good way to find cheaper flights, but check which airport you land at. Getting from Southend to Heathrow for a connection can take longer than some short flights.

The usual mix-ups

People most often confuse LGW and LHR, because both start with L and end with a consonant pair. Remember that Gatwick has the G. The other trap is LCY: the Y comes from the end of "City", not from a place name.

STN and LTN are the next pair to watch. Both end in N, and both are north of London. Think STaNsted and LuToN, and say the letters as you read the name.

A quick memory test

Cover the list above and try to write all six codes from memory. Most people get LHR and LGW straight away, then stall on SEN and LCY. If you can do all six, try our Daily Departure puzzle: London codes turn up often.
