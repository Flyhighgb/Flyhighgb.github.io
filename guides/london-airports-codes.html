London has six airports. Can you name their codes?

LHR, LGW, STN, LTN, LCY and SEN, plus the LON code that covers them all, and how not to mix them up.

London is served by six airports, and their codes are a classic quiz trap because most start with L. Here they are, with a way to remember each.

  • LHR Heathrow: L for London, HR for Heathrow. The big one, in west London.
  • LGW Gatwick: L for London, GW for Gatwick. South of London, in West Sussex.
  • STN Stansted: the letters come straight from STaNsted. North-east of London, in Essex.
  • LTN Luton: LuToN. North of London, in Bedfordshire.
  • LCY London City: London CitY. The closest to central London, in the Docklands.
  • SEN Southend: SouthENd, out east in Essex.

What does LON mean?

LON isn't an airport. It's a city code that covers all of London's airports, so booking sites can search them all at once. Other big cities have one too: NYC for New York, PAR for Paris and TYO for Tokyo.

The usual mix-ups

People most often confuse LGW and LHR, because both start with L and end with a consonant pair. Remember that Gatwick has the G. The other trap is LCY: the Y comes from the end of "City".
