Why does LAX have an X?

Los Angeles is LAX and Portland is PDX. The X doesn't stand for anything, and here's why it's there.

By Amateurgames1 · Published 5 November 2026 · Updated 16 November 2026

Los Angeles is LAX and Portland, Oregon is PDX. In both cases, the X doesn't stand for anything. It's a placeholder, left over from the days when airport codes were shorter.

From two letters to three

In the early days of US aviation, many airports used two-letter codes, borrowed from the weather service, which had already given its stations short identifiers. Los Angeles was LA and Portland was PD. As the number of airports grew, two letters weren't enough to go round, and three-letter codes became the standard. Some airports simply kept their old two letters and added an X to fill the gap.

A code that became a brand

LAX is now so well known that people say it as a word. The airport opened in 1928 as Mines Field, and its space-age Theme Building, which looks like a flying saucer on four legs, has become a symbol of the city. Few people call it Los Angeles International. It's just LAX.

Does X mean anything else?

Not by itself. Plenty of codes contain an X for other reasons, such as KIX for Osaka's Kansai International, where the X helps it stand apart from Osaka's older airport, ITM. Phoenix is PHX, where the X is simply part of the name. If you're guessing, an X at the end of a US code is often a sign of the old two-letter history, but it's not a rule.

Other codes that were stretched

The X wasn't the only way to fill a gap. Some airports added a letter from elsewhere in the name, and others took a completely new code. That's part of why US codes feel less consistent than, say, Canada's, where nearly every code starts with a Y (see why Canadian airports start with Y).

Try it yourself

Next time you see a code ending in X, check whether the first two letters make sense on their own. For LA and PD, they do. It's a small trick, but it turns a random code into one you'll remember.

  • LAX: Los Angeles, from LA.
  • PDX: Portland, Oregon, from PD.
  • KIX: Osaka Kansai, a different story.
  • PHX: Phoenix, where the X is in the name.

Not to be confused with Portland, Maine, which is PWM. If you're flying to Oregon, make sure the tag says PDX.
