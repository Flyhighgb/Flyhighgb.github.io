City codes: LON, NYC, PAR and the cities with several airports

LON isn't an airport. Neither is NYC or TYO. Here's what city codes are, the main ones, and the airports behind them.

By Amateurgames1 · Published 16 November 2026

Type LON into a flight search and you'll get flights to six different airports. That's because LON is a city code: a three-letter code that stands for a whole city rather than one airport. Big cities with more than one airport usually have one.

Why city codes exist

If you want to fly to London and don't mind which airport, searching each one separately is slow. A city code lets a booking site search them all at once. You'll never see a city code on a bag tag, though. Your bag always goes to a specific airport.

The main city codes

  • LON, London: LHR, LGW, STN, LTN, LCY and SEN.
  • NYC, New York: JFK, LGA and EWR (Newark, across the river in New Jersey).
  • PAR, Paris: CDG and ORY.
  • TYO, Tokyo: HND and NRT.
  • OSA, Osaka: KIX and ITM.
  • SEL, Seoul: ICN and GMP.
  • BJS, Beijing: PEK and PKX.
  • MIL, Milan: MXP and LIN.
  • ROM, Rome: FCO and CIA.
  • STO, Stockholm: ARN and the city's smaller airports.
  • CHI, Chicago: ORD and MDW.
  • WAS, Washington: IAD, DCA and BWI.
  • BUE, Buenos Aires: EZE and AEP.
  • SAO, São Paulo: GRU and Congonhas.
  • YTO, Toronto: YYZ and Billy Bishop.
  • YMQ, Montréal: YUL and the city's other airports.

When the city code is also an airport

Not every city has a separate city code. In some places the main airport's code doubles as the city code. Shanghai is a good example of how this gets confusing: SHA is the code for Hongqiao airport, but Shanghai's bigger airport is PVG, Pudong. Always check the airport, not just the city.

Watch out when connecting

City codes are great for finding flights but can catch you out on connections. If you land at Gatwick and your next flight leaves from Heathrow, both are "LON", but they're about an hour apart by road. The same goes for JFK and Newark, Haneda and Narita, or Orly and Charles de Gaulle. Airlines usually won't move your bag between them for you.

Airports that borrow a big city's name

Some airports far outside a city are marketed under its name, such as Paris Beauvais (BVA) or Brussels South Charleroi (CRL). These usually aren't part of the city code. Our guide to airports named for a city they're not in covers them.
