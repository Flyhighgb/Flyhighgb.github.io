Airports named for a city they're not in

Paris Beauvais, Brussels South Charleroi, Milan Bergamo. Some airports borrow a big city's name from a long way away. Here's how far.

By Amateurgames1 · Published 16 November 2026

Low-cost airlines love a smaller airport: cheaper fees, quicker turnarounds, less congestion. To make those airports easier to sell, they're often marketed under the name of the nearest big city, even when that city is a long bus ride away. The code is the giveaway.

Europe's best-known examples

  • BVA Paris Beauvais: next to the town of Beauvais, around 80 km north of Paris. Paris's main airports are CDG and ORY.
  • CRL Brussels South Charleroi: in Charleroi, a separate city about 50 km south of Brussels. Brussels's own airport is BRU.
  • BGY Milan Bergamo: in Bergamo, about 45 km north-east of Milan. Milan's own airports are MXP and LIN.
  • Frankfurt-Hahn (HHN): over 100 km west of Frankfurt by road, nowhere near the main airport, FRA.
  • Stockholm Skavsta (NYO): about 100 km south of Stockholm, near Nyköping, which is where the code comes from. Stockholm's main airport is ARN.
  • Barcelona Girona (GRO): near Girona, about 90 km north of Barcelona. Barcelona's own airport is BCN.

Closer than they sound

Some airports with a borrowed name are reasonably close. London Stansted (STN) and Luton (LTN) are outside London but have fast trains. Rome Ciampino (CIA) is actually closer to central Rome than Fiumicino. Tokyo Narita (NRT) is about 60 km from the city but well served by express trains.

How to spot one

  • Look at the code. If it doesn't match the city, and you don't recognise it as the city's main airport, look it up.
  • Check whether the airport is included in the city code (PAR, MIL, STO and so on). Airports far outside the city usually aren't.
  • Look up the transfer. A cheap flight can lose its appeal if the coach into town costs nearly as much and takes over an hour.

Why it matters for connections

Booking two separate cheap flights through different airports in the same "city" is risky. Getting from Beauvais to Charles de Gaulle, or from Bergamo to Malpensa, can take two hours or more. Leave plenty of time, or keep your journey to one airport.
