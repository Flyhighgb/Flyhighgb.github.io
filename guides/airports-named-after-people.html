Airports named after people, and the codes that ignore them

Charles de Gaulle got his initials. John Lennon, Chopin and Galileo didn't. Who airports are named after, and why most codes don't show it.

By Amateurgames1 · Published 16 November 2026

Many airports are named after a famous person, but you'd rarely know it from the code. Usually the name came after the code was set, and the code stayed. Only a few airports carry their person's initials on every bag tag.

The ones that kept the name in the code

  • CDG: Paris Charles de Gaulle, after the French wartime leader and president. The airport opened in 1974.
  • JFK: New York, after President John F. Kennedy. The airport was Idlewild (IDL) until it was renamed in 1963, one of the rare times a code was changed to match.
  • LGA: New York LaGuardia, after Fiorello La Guardia, the city's mayor from 1934 to 1945.

Named after leaders

  • DEL: Delhi, Indira Gandhi International.
  • JNB: Johannesburg, O.R. Tambo, after the anti-apartheid leader Oliver Tambo.
  • NBO: Nairobi, Jomo Kenyatta, Kenya's first president.
  • DAR: Dar es Salaam, Julius Nyerere, Tanzania's first president.
  • TLV: Tel Aviv, Ben Gurion, Israel's first prime minister.
  • DCA: Washington, Ronald Reagan Washington National.
  • IAD: Washington Dulles, after John Foster Dulles, US Secretary of State.
  • IAH: Houston, George Bush Intercontinental.
  • MNL: Manila, Ninoy Aquino, the opposition politician shot at the airport in 1983.
  • LIS: Lisbon, Humberto Delgado, a general who stood against the Salazar dictatorship.

Named after artists, scientists and writers

  • LPL: Liverpool John Lennon. Its motto is "Above us only sky".
  • WAW: Warsaw Chopin, after the composer.
  • PRG: Prague Václav Havel, the playwright who became president.
  • FCO: Rome Fiumicino, officially Leonardo da Vinci.
  • PSA: Pisa, Galileo Galilei, who was born in the city.
  • VCE: Venice Marco Polo, after the Venetian traveller.
  • LYS: Lyon Saint-Exupéry, after the pilot and author of The Little Prince.
  • BEG: Belgrade Nikola Tesla, after the inventor.
  • MSY: New Orleans Louis Armstrong, after the jazz musician.

Named after aviators

  • SYD: Sydney Kingsford Smith, after the pilot who first flew across the Pacific from the US to Australia in 1928.
  • ORD: Chicago O'Hare, after the naval aviator Butch O'Hare.
  • SAW: Istanbul Sabiha Gökçen, often called the world's first female fighter pilot.
  • MKE: Milwaukee Mitchell, after General Billy Mitchell, an early champion of air power.

And a few surprises

  • SNA: Orange County John Wayne, after the actor.
  • BHD: George Best Belfast City, after the footballer.
  • LAS: Las Vegas Harry Reid, renamed in 2021 after the Nevada senator.
  • HNL: Honolulu Daniel K. Inouye, after Hawaii's long-serving senator.

Why the codes don't follow

An airport code is built into airline systems, timetables and baggage machines, so changing it is expensive and risky. Renaming an airport is just a new sign. That's why Las Vegas stayed LAS when McCarran became Harry Reid, and why Liverpool stayed LPL. JFK is the famous exception. Read more in who decides airport codes.
