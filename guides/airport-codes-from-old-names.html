Airport codes that remember an old name

ORD for O'Hare, MCO for Orlando, BOM for Mumbai. These codes kept the name the airport or city had before.

By Amateurgames1 · Published 5 November 2026 · Updated 16 November 2026

Airport codes rarely change, even when an airport or city gets a new name. A new code would mean updating airline systems, timetables, signs and bag-tag printers all over the world, so the old letters usually stay. That turns some codes into little history lessons. Here are the best-known ones.

Chicago O'Hare: ORD

O'Hare started life as Orchard Field, next to an aircraft factory. It was renamed in 1949 after Butch O'Hare, a US Navy fighter pilot and Medal of Honor recipient, but the code ORD, from ORcharD, stayed. Chicago's other airport, MDW, is named after the Battle of Midway.

Orlando: MCO

Orlando's main airport was once McCoy Air Force Base. The base is long gone, but MCO lives on, and millions of theme-park visitors land on its letters every year.

New Orleans: MSY

MSY comes from Moisant Stock Yards, the site of the original airfield, named after the aviator John Moisant. Today the airport is named after the jazz musician Louis Armstrong, but the code still remembers the stockyards.

Cincinnati: CVG

Cincinnati's airport is actually in Kentucky, across the Ohio River near the town of Covington. Hence CVG.

Kansas City and Nashville: MCI and BNA

MCI comes from Mid-Continent International, Kansas City's airport's name when it opened. BNA comes from Berry Field, Nashville, the original name of the city's airport.

New York JFK: JFK

This one did change, which shows how unusual that is. The airport opened as Idlewild, with the code IDL. When it was renamed after President John F. Kennedy in December 1963, the code changed to JFK too.

Cities that changed their names

Asia has the most examples, because many cities moved from an older English spelling to a local one after the codes were set.

  • PEK: Beijing, from Peking, the older English spelling. Beijing's newer airport, PKX, echoes it.
  • BOM: Mumbai, from Bombay. The city was renamed in 1995.
  • MAA: Chennai, from Madras. The city was renamed in 1996.
  • CCU: Kolkata, from Calcutta. The city was renamed in 2001.
  • BLR: Bengaluru, from Bangalore. The official name changed in 2014.
  • COK: Kochi, from Cochin.
  • RGN: Yangon, from Rangoon.
  • SGN: Ho Chi Minh City, from Saigon, a name locals still use.
  • OPO: Porto, from Oporto, its traditional English name.

Russia has a famous one too. St Petersburg's airport is still LED, from Leningrad, the city's name from 1924 to 1991.

How to use this when you're guessing

If a code makes no sense for the city, try the old name. Indian and Southeast Asian codes very often follow it. In the US, try the name of the airfield or the town next door. And if neither works, it may be a code that had to dodge a clash, which is a story for another guide.
